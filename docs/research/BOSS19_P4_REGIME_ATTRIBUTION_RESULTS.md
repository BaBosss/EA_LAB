## 3. Interpretation (what the evidence supports — no more)

1. **Is Boss19's edge concentrated in a specific regime?** Partially. NEUTRAL and RISK_OFF macro states, combined with TREND_UP local structure, carry the large majority of positive P&L and participation. But this is not a clean single-regime story: NEUTRAL alone is 66% of all units and a majority of net, simply because NEUTRAL is the most common macro state in the sample — high participation does not by itself mean the edge is *concentrated* there in a differential sense. RISK_OFF has a much higher pooled PF (6.39) on far fewer units (286), which is a more distinctive but thinner signal, and even that signal is symbol-concentrated (XAUUSD carries most of it).
2. **Does the positive MAIN pulse show regime concentration?** Directionally yes but overstated in an earlier draft: MAIN's positive result is not evenly spread across regime tuples (TREND_UP and NEUTRAL/RISK_OFF dominate it), but this data cannot separate "MAIN has a different regime *mix* than BWD" from "the *same* regime performs differently within MAIN than within BWD" — both are visibly present (e.g. MAIN×NEUTRAL is +11,092.89 vs BWD×NEUTRAL is −328.58, a sign-relevant swing for the *same* label), so composition alone does not account for the MAIN/BWD gap.
3. **Does regime composition explain BWD weakness? (corrected after independent review)** Only partially, and the earlier "substantially" framing was an overstatement. The BWD-ex-STRESS arithmetic (net +161 once STRESS is removed) is real, but a year-stratified breakdown shows STRESS is not a stable underperformer: it is strongly positive in `BWD-2020` (net +753.34, n=59, PF 7.44) and in `MAIN-2025` (net +987.49, n=27, PF 3.54), and only sharply negative in `BWD-2022` (net −2,015.27, n=57, PF 0.39). The more accurate statement is that **2022 specifically was a bad year for STRESS-classified entries**, not that classifier-STRESS is generally harmful — the regime label and the calendar year are confounded in this sample, and this evidence cannot separate them.
4. **Is the same regime label behaviorally consistent across symbol/TF/window?** No, not uniformly. Several regime tuples flip sign between MAIN and BWD with workable sample sizes on both sides (see Sec. 2), and the STRESS macro state itself flips sign across years within the same window (item 3). Pooled-regime performance also depends heavily on which symbol supplies most of the units within that regime (RISK_OFF ≈ XAUUSD-driven; NEUTRAL ≈ XAUUSD-driven at 60.6% of its net, with GBPUSD/AUDUSD flat-to-negative within NEUTRAL).
5. **Sign reversal / context dependence?** Yes, present and non-trivial, though these are related views of overlapping data, not independent confirmations: the `(STRESS, TREND_UP, EXTREME)` tuple is a subset of the STRESS macro state, and its MAIN-vs-BWD split (Sec. 2) draws on the same underlying STRESS observations as the year-stratified breakdown (item 3) — together they are one consistent picture of STRESS-related non-stationarity seen from a few angles, not three separate proofs of it. Two genuinely distinct tuples do still flip sign between MAIN and BWD with workable n on both sides outside of STRESS (`(NEUTRAL, TREND_UP, LOW)` and `(RISK_OFF, TREND_UP, NORMAL)`, Sec. 2), so context dependence is not solely a STRESS/2022 artifact.
6. **Overall dependence shape:** **Mixed, and more precisely time-confounded than "mixed" alone conveys.** The evidence does not support a single-axis story ("Boss19 is a regime-X edge," "a symbol-Y edge," or "STRESS is a bad regime"). The most defensible single statement is: Boss19's realized result in this sample is the joint, non-separable product of window composition, symbol mix (XAUUSD weight), and calendar year — and at least one candidate regime-level explanation (STRESS→BWD weakness) that looked clean at the window-pooled level dissolves into a single-year effect once year-stratified.
7. **Information value for P5?** Present but weaker than an earlier draft claimed, and the STRESS hypothesis specifically is not yet P5-ready as stated (see Sec. 6). What the evidence does support as a P5 input is narrower: "is Boss19's 2022 weakness a calendar-year effect, a STRESS-regime effect, or both, and can they be told apart with more/different data?" — a diagnostic question, not yet an actionable filter/kill-switch hypothesis. The regime×symbol concentration findings (item 1, item 4) are informative context but do not themselves support a specific regime-restriction recommendation at current, uneven participation.

## 4. Bias / scrutiny check

- **Selection bias / hindsight (classifier/timeline construction):** Not evident *at the classifier/timeline level*, and this narrower scope is a deliberate correction after independent review, which noted the original "not evident" claim was stated too broadly. What is independently verifiable from *this evidence chain's own git history* (not from the CSVs alone): the classifier (`BOSS19_P4_REGIME_CLASSIFIER_V1`, contract base `b7c0d624`, established ~2026-08-30) and its timeline hash (`5f3a0f8d1accd25...`, first pinned in commit `2fc0055f` on 2026-09-01, unchanged across 9 subsequent commits over 2 days) both predate the broad36 package (`ecd5c2b3`, 2026-09-02) and this join (`353ab806`, 2026-09-02/03) by days; commit `2fc0055f`'s own contemporaneous text states H3 outcome bytes were opened only after the timeline gate was locked; the classifier reads only market OHLC, never Boss19 P&L. That structurally prevents outcome-driven *threshold* tuning. It does **not** prevent outcome-driven *narrative* selection at the interpretation stage — see the next bullet, which is the more relevant bias risk for this document specifically.
- **Post-hoc selection of which regime to feature as a P5 hypothesis:** A real risk that the original draft did not name. STRESS was picked out for the BWD-weakness narrative *after* viewing all 547 affinity partitions and finding it was the one macro state with a negative pooled net — exactly the "isolated winner" pattern this document's own multiple-comparisons discipline is supposed to guard against. The year-stratified breakdown in Sec. 2/3 item 3 (STRESS positive in `BWD-2020` and `MAIN-2025`, negative only in `BWD-2022`) is the direct consequence of taking that risk seriously and checking it, and it substantially weakens the original narrative rather than confirming it.
- **Multiple comparisons:** No single one of the 547 fine-grained affinity cells is treated as a significant finding in isolation. However, independent review correctly noted the original claim that every conclusion was "cross-checked across at least two of {window, symbol, year}" did not hold for the STRESS→BWD hypothesis specifically until the year-stratified check above was added — that check is now part of this document, not merely claimed.
- **Concentration:** Explicitly surfaced throughout Sec. 2–3, not hidden: XAUUSD dominance (60.6% of NEUTRAL's own net, 65.3% of RISK_OFF's), the STRESS/BWD-2022 year-confound, `SHORT_CALENDAR_SPAN` on 31% of partitions, `SINGLE_TUPLE_*` flags on 11% of partitions.
- **Participation:** Explicitly surfaced in relative terms in Sec. 2 (no universal numeric floor is invoked, per contract §4) — the largest pooled cells (MAIN×NEUTRAL n=532, BWD×NEUTRAL n=496) are visibly larger than the thinnest ones used in the STRESS narrative (BWD-2022×STRESS n=57, BWD-2020×STRESS n=59, MAIN-2025×STRESS n=27) — none of the STRESS year-slices are large populations.
- **Negative evidence retained:** STRESS macro state (pooled net −274.44, PF 0.93), BWD window overall (PF 0.89), years 2021/2022 (both net negative), AUDUSD (flat/near-zero), the `(STRESS, TREND_UP, EXTREME)` tuple (largest single loss bucket), and — added after independent review — the fact that STRESS is *positive* in two of its three largest year-slices, which cuts against treating it as a uniformly bad regime, are all reported here rather than omitted for being unfavorable to any single narrative (positive or negative).
