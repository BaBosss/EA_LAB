# Academic abnormal return can disappear when implementation constraints are applied
## SOURCE_CLAIM
Bushee and Raedy simulate seven documented stock-market strategies while adding real-world constraints such as transaction/price-impact costs, short-sale restrictions, portfolio concentration and ownership limits. In their 1990–2002 simulations, size and return-reversal strategies weaken materially, while cash-flow-to-price, return momentum and post-earnings-announcement drift remain more resilient across the tested constraints.
## Method / context
- US equity portfolios; repeated random initial portfolios
- Active management through 1990–2002
- Price impact varies with trade size, price, market cap and listing venue
- Fund size, number of holdings and allocation method materially affect implementability
## Result

Under the tested implementation constraints, size and return-reversal strategies weakened materially, while cash-flow-to-price, return momentum, and post-earnings-announcement drift remained comparatively resilient; price impact and portfolio/ownership constraints materially reduced achievable returns.

## Limitations / caveats
The study targets institutional stock portfolios and historical US market structure; its exact cost model does not equal retail MT5 spread/slippage. The paper intentionally focuses on implementability rather than re-solving the underlying risk-premium debate.
## EA_LAB_INFERENCE
Backtest profitability and deployable profitability are separate claims. Strategy synthesis and Factory validation should include cost/liquidity/turnover assumptions early enough to kill ideas whose edge depends on frictionless execution.
