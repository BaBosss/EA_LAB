# High-and-rising inflation is a distinct environment with different strategy behavior

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Neville et al. study passive and active strategies across the U.S., U.K. and Japan using roughly 95 years of history. They define inflationary regimes quantitatively as periods in which year-over-year headline inflation is accelerating and reaches at least 5%.

The source reports poor real performance for traditional nominal bonds and equities during high-and-rising inflation regimes. Commodities perform better in aggregate, while among the dynamic strategies time-series trend following provides the most reliable protection in the historical inflation shocks studied.

The paper argues that inflation shocks often unfold as prolonged episodes rather than overnight events, which can suit trend strategies. It also cautions that bitcoin's short available history does not establish it as a reliable inflation hedge.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: U.S., U.K. and Japan; equities, fixed income, commodities, factors, trend strategies and alternatives
- Timeframe / horizon: monthly / macro-regime horizon
- Sample period: from 1926; 34 inflation episodes across three countries, including eight U.S. regimes
- Method: quantitatively defined high-and-rising inflation regimes; real-return comparison across passive and active strategies
- Comparison / benchmark: inflationary versus non-inflationary regimes
## Result

The source's historical evidence supports treating inflation state as a meaningful conditioning variable: strategy and asset behavior differs materially during high-and-rising inflation episodes, and trend following was comparatively resilient in those episodes.

## Limitations / caveats

This is macro/portfolio evidence, not proof that an intraday EA should trade CPI directly or that an inflation label predicts next-bar returns. Historical regime definitions depend on long-run macro data and the tested assets/strategies. Capacity and implementation differ across strategy types.
