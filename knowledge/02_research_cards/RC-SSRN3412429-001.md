# RSI extremes can encode trend consistency and momentum rather than only reversal

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Hill argues that RSI should not be treated only as an overbought/oversold reversal indicator. The paper tests RSI as both a trend-consistency and momentum measure on historical S&P 500 constituents.

The tested bullish range requires 14-day RSI to stay between 40 and 100 over a lookback window; bullish momentum requires RSI to exceed 70 within the window. The combined bull range-momentum signal therefore asks for both persistent trend structure and evidence of strong upside momentum.

The source reports that longer bull range-momentum signals, especially 75-125 day windows, occupy the stronger Success Rate / Profit-Loss area in its tests. RSI values above 70 are interpreted as exceptional upside strength in this momentum context rather than automatically as a sell condition.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: historical constituents of the S&P 500
- Timeframe / horizon: daily price data; 14-day RSI; 25/50/75/100/125 trading-day signal windows
- Sample period: 1998-07-01 through 2018-07-02
- Method: bull/bear RSI ranges, momentum thresholds, and combined range-momentum signals
- Comparison / benchmark: Success Rate, Average Advance/Decline and Profit/Loss Ratio across signal definitions/lookbacks
## Result

Within this S&P 500 daily study, a sustained RSI range plus recurring high-RSI momentum carried more useful information than range alone for identifying strong uptrends.

## Limitations / caveats

The paper states that commissions and slippage are not considered. The sample is U.S. equities/daily data and uses historical index membership to reduce survivorship bias. The reported success metrics are not evidence that the same RSI levels or lookback durations are optimal on XAU, FX or crypto intraday data.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

RSI semantics should be strategy-family dependent. `RSI > 70` can be a momentum-continuation feature in a trend hypothesis rather than a universal reversal trigger. A future EA experiment should test the semantic role explicitly and include execution costs, regime split and asset/timeframe transfer checks.
