# RSI thresholds can be used as trend-state boundaries, not only 30/70 reversal triggers
## SOURCE_CLAIM
Panigrahi, Vachhani and Chaudhury study RSI-based trend identification on the NIFTY 50 and compare two modified threshold schemes described as 50-50 and 60-40. Their abstract reports the 50-50 approach producing higher long-term return while 60-40 performs better over shorter horizons in their analysis.
## Method / context
- Market: NIFTY 50
- RSI used as a momentum/trend indicator
- Data: secondary market data with TradingView/Excel analysis
- Study discusses conventional 30/70 interpretation alongside trend-oriented center/range logic
## Result

For the NIFTY 50 analysis reported by the authors, the 50-50 RSI threshold scheme produced the higher long-term return, while the 60-40 scheme produced the stronger short-term result. The finding is specific to the study's index, period, and threshold definitions.

## Limitations / caveats
The paper is narrow to one equity index and its threshold constructions. It does not establish that any threshold is portable to XAU, FX, crypto or intraday MT5 execution, and literature summarized inside the paper includes mixed RSI findings.
## EA_LAB_INFERENCE
RSI semantics must be declared by strategy family and horizon before testing. A numeric RSI level is not a universal action command; it may represent reversal, trend state or momentum depending on the hypothesis.
