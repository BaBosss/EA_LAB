# Multiple testing can turn a random strategy into an apparently strong winner

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Harvey and Liu argue that trading-strategy evaluation must adjust for the number of strategies or strategy combinations tried. A selected strategy's reported Sharpe Ratio and conventional significance can be overstated when it is the winner of many tests.

Their illustrative candidate has Sharpe 0.92 and t-statistic 2.91, yet it is the best of 200 daily strategies generated from zero-mean random numbers. Under their multiple-testing treatment the strategy is rejected rather than accepted as genuine skill.

The paper discusses Family-Wise Error Rate and False Discovery Rate approaches, including Bonferroni, Holm and BHY. In the 200-test illustration, Bonferroni reduces the Sharpe-equivalent estimate from 0.92 to 0.08, a 91% haircut. The authors also discuss out-of-sample testing and Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) as complementary approaches.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: trading-strategy evaluation; examples include simulated strategies and an empirical strategy set
- Timeframe / horizon: mixed; significance linked to return observation count and years
- Sample period: paper version 2014; examples are methodological rather than one EA market
- Method: multiple-hypothesis testing; FWER/FDR procedures; Sharpe/t-statistic adjustment; comparison with OOS/PBO
- Comparison / benchmark: conventional single-test significance versus multiple-test-adjusted significance
## Result

A visually attractive or statistically significant best backtest is not sufficient evidence when many alternatives were searched. The testing history changes the evidentiary hurdle.

## Limitations / caveats

The authors state that Sharpe Ratio can be inappropriate for non-normal return distributions and that their treatment focuses on individual strategies rather than the portfolio problem. A multiple-testing correction also depends on what tests were actually tried; an incomplete experiment history weakens the correction.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

Factory search breadth itself is evidence-relevant. Parameter sweeps, strategy variants, symbols and timeframes should not be treated as independent invisible attempts when interpreting a selected winner.
