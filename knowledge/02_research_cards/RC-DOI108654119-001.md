# Leading indicators are macro-state evidence, not a trading signal by themselves

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Stock and Watson (1989) construct three experimental U.S. business-cycle indexes using econometric methods: a coincident index based on a dynamic-factor model, a leading index intended to forecast growth in the coincident index over a six-month horizon, and a recession index estimating the probability of recession six months ahead.

The paper reports that interest-rate variables, including a public-private risk premium and the yield-curve slope, were particularly useful predictors in the proposed leading index.

## Method / context

- Primary locator: DOI `10.1086/654119`; NBER Macroeconomics Annual 1989.
- Market/context: U.S. macroeconomic activity, not asset returns.
- Forecast horizon in the leading/recession indexes: six months.
- Model role: latent/current-state measurement plus forward macro forecasting.

## Result

The source is a methodological precedent for separating current-state estimation from forward-state forecasting. It does not show that its variables directly predict FX, XAU, BTC, or EA profitability.

## Limitations / caveats

The study is historical U.S. macro research and predates the intended EA_LAB markets, data feeds and execution environment. Later data revisions and real-time availability matter if such variables are ever used prospectively.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

The useful transfer is architectural: a regime system should distinguish `state now` from `forecast of state later`, freeze data vintages/as-of timing, and test whether either adds incremental information to strategy evidence.

Do not add yield-curve slope, credit spread or any other named variable to Boss19 P4A from this card. P4A is frozen, and any later macro-feature change requires a separate prospective hypothesis.
