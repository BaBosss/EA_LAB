# Simple price-based trend following as a risk-management overlay

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Faber's tactical asset-allocation study presents a simple non-discretionary trend-following method intended primarily to manage risk rather than optimize a complex forecasting model. The design criteria are mechanical logic, the same model and parameters across asset classes, and price-only inputs.

The paper uses a long-horizon moving-average market-timing approach, centered on a 10-month simple moving average, first on U.S. equities and then across multiple asset classes. The 2013 update reports that the monthly approach since 1973 produced higher risk-adjusted results and materially reduced exposure to prolonged bear markets in the tested allocation framework.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: U.S. equities plus foreign equities, government bonds, commodities and REITs; later extensions add assets
- Timeframe / horizon: monthly system / long-horizon allocation
- Sample period: long U.S. history plus multi-asset allocation analysis from 1973 through 2012 in the update
- Method: price relative to long moving average; mechanical enter/exit risk overlay; diversified allocation
- Comparison / benchmark: buy-and-hold and alternative portfolio allocations
## Result

The source's conclusion is that a simple non-discretionary trend model can act as a risk-reduction technique in the studied monthly asset-allocation setting while avoiding many large, prolonged bear-market losses.

## Limitations / caveats

This is an asset-allocation / monthly-horizon study, not evidence that the same moving-average rule transfers to intraday XAU, FX or BTC EAs. Reported historical performance includes the source's own data, allocation, cash, and implementation assumptions; those must not be silently imported into an EA.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

The reusable idea is structural simplicity: a trend mechanism can be framed as an operating-state/risk filter using stable price-based rules before adding indicators. For EA_LAB this is a hypothesis source for trend/regime design, not permission to copy the 10-month threshold into MT5.
