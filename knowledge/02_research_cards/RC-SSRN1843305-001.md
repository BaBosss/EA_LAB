# Finite execution time can rationally increase order aggressiveness

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Fano and Pellizzari model a continuous double auction with heterogeneous buyers and sellers who privately know values or costs and have limited time to transact. Approximate equilibrium strategies are computed using evolution strategies.

The model finds that equilibrium bids and asks become increasingly aggressive as the trading session progresses, following an approximately linear schedule. The mechanism is the immediacy-efficacy trade-off: more aggressive prices increase execution probability while reducing gain conditional on execution.

The model makes missed execution economically relevant because traders have a finite opportunity to transact and must condition behavior on their place in the sequence and on how many traders remain.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: stylized continuous double auction, not a specific traded instrument
- Timeframe / horizon: finite trading session / order-submission sequence
- Sample period: simulation/model study rather than historical market sample
- Method: stochastic-game equilibrium approximated with evolution strategies
- Comparison / benchmark: alternative time-dependent bidding/asking strategies
## Result

The source supports a microstructure principle that optimal execution aggressiveness may depend on remaining execution opportunity rather than being constant through time.

## Limitations / caveats

The model is intentionally stylized: unit orders, one submission opportunity, no cancellation/resubmission within a session, random arrival order and simplified information structure. It does not establish that a particular MT5 order schedule is profitable in real markets and does not measure broker spread, slippage or queue priority in the EA_LAB environment.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

Execution logic should be evaluated separately from entry alpha. A future bounded execution study may test whether time-to-deadline or missed-execution risk justifies controlled changes in order aggressiveness, but this paper does not authorize runtime execution changes or supply production parameters.
