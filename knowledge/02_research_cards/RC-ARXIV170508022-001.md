# Macro forecasts can be a conditional layer for mean reversion, not a free edge

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Sharma (2017) starts from mean-reverting yield-curve ideas, implements a multiple-pairs strategy on major currency pairs, and then incorporates machine-learning forecasts of macroeconomic variables by optimizing weights on trading signals.

The paper reports a clear APR improvement over its evaluation period after adding the macroeconomic forecast layer. The source therefore presents macro variables as potentially useful alongside technical signals in that specific experiment.

## Method / context

- Primary locator: arXiv `1705.08022`; SSRN `2972365`.
- Market / asset: major currency pairs, motivated by yield-curve mean reversion.
- Added layer: machine-learning forecasts of pertinent macro variables.
- Evidence checked: primary/publication abstract and metadata.

## Result

The source is relevant to EA_LAB because it combines FX mean reversion with macro context. It does not establish that macro forecasting universally improves FX strategies or that its optimized signal weights transfer to another dataset, broker, horizon, or pair universe.

## Limitations / caveats

The positive result is one study and the macro layer itself uses optimized weights. That creates a direct selection/overfit concern for EA_LAB. The paper does not authorize importing its pair set, model, weights, forecast horizon, or acceptance metric.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

If `HYP-SB-003` later establishes a deterministic net-cost pairs baseline, macro context can become a separate one-logical-change child. The child must freeze macro features, as-of timing, forecast construction and weighting before outcome evidence.

Macro data must remain versioned/as-of and cannot be backfilled with revised future values. BWD/HOLDOUT must not be used to tune the macro layer.
