# Relative-strength rotation is distinct from single-asset trend following

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Faber studies monthly relative-strength ranking across ten U.S. equity sectors using French-Fama data from the late 1920s through 2009, and later across a global multi-asset universe. Each month assets are ranked by trailing total return over one-to-twelve-month lookbacks or a combination of horizons, and the portfolio holds the top-ranked subset.

The report states that the relative-strength portfolios outperform the buy-and-hold benchmark in roughly 70% of years across the tested settings, with a rough 300-600 basis-point annual outperformance estimate. The paper also emphasizes that the long-only rotation portfolios retain substantial volatility and drawdown exposure.

A dynamic hedge using a 10-month simple moving average and broader asset-class diversification are presented as methods that reduce volatility and drawdown while preserving much of the reported return profile.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: U.S. equity sectors; later global asset classes
- Timeframe / horizon: monthly ranking/rebalance; 1, 3, 6, 9, 12-month and combined lookbacks
- Sample period: U.S. sector study roughly 1928-2009; global asset-class study from 1973
- Method: cross-sectional relative-strength ranking with top-X portfolio selection; optional trend hedge
- Comparison / benchmark: equal-weight/buy-and-hold portfolios and hedged versus unhedged variants
## Result

The source supports a cross-sectional rotation mechanism in which relative ranking among assets is the selection rule, separate from single-asset time-series trend logic.

## Limitations / caveats

The paper notes real-world implementation frictions. Base results exclude taxes, commissions and slippage; historical turnover can be high, and some early sector exposures would not have been practically investable through modern funds. The author also notes that taxes can materially reduce realized performance. Long-only relative strength alone does not remove market beta or large drawdown risk.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

Relative-strength rotation should be modeled as its own strategy family with an explicit ranking universe, rebalance cadence, turnover budget and execution-cost model. The positive long-horizon evidence here should be evaluated alongside market-specific contradictory evidence such as the Irish momentum study rather than treated as universal. This card does not authorize portfolio automation or risk/default changes.
