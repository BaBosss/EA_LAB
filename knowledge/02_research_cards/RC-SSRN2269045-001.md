# Irish equity momentum provides a counterexample to universal momentum claims

## SOURCE_CLAIM

O'Sullivan and O'Sullivan examine momentum portfolios in the Irish equity market over 1988-2007 using alternative backward-looking ranking periods, forward holding horizons and portfolio sizes. They report that momentum portfolio returns are highly non-normal and serially correlated, raising concern about standard parametric significance tests.

The study therefore uses bootstrap nonparametric p-values for abnormal-performance statistics and Newey-West adjusted standard errors. Over the full sample, the authors find very little evidence that momentum strategies generated abnormal risk-adjusted returns.

Some profitable cases appear in a relatively high market-growth subperiod, but the authors describe those conditions as comparatively rare and their persistence as unreliable; their overall conclusion remains against abnormal momentum returns in this market.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: Irish listed equities
- Timeframe / horizon: monthly ranking and holding horizons across multiple configurations
- Sample period: approximately 1988-2007
- Method: winner-minus-loser momentum portfolios; factor-adjusted alpha; bootstrap nonparametric p-values; Newey-West errors
- Comparison / benchmark: alternative ranking/holding horizons and portfolio sizes
## Result

The paper is direct contradictory evidence against treating momentum profitability as universal across markets. Its methodological contribution is also relevant: non-normal strategy returns can change inference when standard t-tests are replaced or supplemented by resampling-based tests.

## Limitations / caveats

The evidence is specific to the Irish equity market and its sample period; it does not disprove momentum in other markets, horizons or structures. Some subperiod/configuration results are positive, so the paper supports conditionality rather than a universal negative conclusion.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

A future EA_LAB momentum hypothesis should state market, horizon and regime explicitly and should not rely on one parametric significance statistic when returns are materially non-normal or serially dependent. This card is negative/contradictory research evidence only; it is not a failed EA experiment and must not become an independent negative-experiment registry.
