# Transaction regions couple pair timing, size and proportional costs

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Xing models pairs trading under a cointegrated relationship between two stock log-prices with proportional transaction costs. Unlike approaches that optimize only share count or only timing, the singular stochastic-control formulation jointly determines trading times and number of shares.

The paper develops a discrete-time dynamic-programming method to compute transaction regions and reports an out-of-sample empirical study using six U.S. stock pairs from different sectors.

## Method / context

- Primary locator: arXiv `1911.10450`; published DOI `10.3390/jrfm15040147`.
- Market / asset: U.S. equity pairs.
- Relationship assumption: cointegrated log prices.
- Cost model: proportional transaction costs.
- Decision object: transaction regions governing when and how much to trade.

## Result

The source supports the idea that pair entry/exit timing, position magnitude and costs are coupled decisions. Its positive empirical result is source-specific and does not establish transfer to retail FX, XAU, BTC or MT5 execution.

## Limitations / caveats

The transaction-region solution depends on the assumed stochastic model, cost structure and utility objective. EA_LAB should not import those assumptions as defaults. A mathematically optimal policy under a misspecified relationship model can still fail when the relationship breaks.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

For `HYP-SB-003`, first prove a simple deterministic relationship and cost-aware baseline. Only then consider transaction-region logic as an advanced child. The child would need one frozen objective and one frozen cost model, with parent-versus-child evidence on the same data identity.

This paper strengthens the requirement to measure both leg sizing and timing, but it does not justify optimizing them together before the baseline mechanism is established.
