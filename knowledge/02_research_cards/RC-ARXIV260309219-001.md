# AlgoXpert: stable regions, purged WFA, execution-aware gates

Primary reference: Pham, Nguyen & Nguyen, arXiv:2603.09219 (2026).

## SOURCE_CLAIM

The paper proposes a chronological three-stage validation protocol:
`IS stability mapping -> purged rolling WFA -> strict OOS under parameter lock`.

In IS, the paper argues for selecting stable parameter regions rather than a single extreme optimum and introduces neighboring-parameter cliff checks. During WFA it restricts degrees of freedom, inserts a purge gap between each train and forward-test segment, resets state before the test segment, applies precommitted fold benchmarks, uses a majority-pass rule and catastrophic veto, and reports train-to-test degradation as diagnostic information.

The framework also embeds execution/risk constraints and stress testing rather than appending them only after signal optimization. Examples include spread/leverage guards, adverse execution assumptions, circuit breakers and a kill switch. It proposes safeguard ablation to measure the marginal effect of protection layers.

The empirical illustration uses a stateful intraday USDJPY M5 strategy and emphasizes grid/trailing/inventory-like path dependence as a reason that naive chronological splitting can still leak state.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: time-series trading; case study USDJPY M5.
- Horizon: intraday case study; framework intended more broadly for stateful/path-dependent strategies.
- Method: parameter stability mapping, restricted rolling WFA with purge/state reset, strict OOS, execution/risk stress.
- Decision design: paper-specific pass/fail gates and `Deploy / Reject / Refactor` outcomes.

## Result

The source presents the framework as a way to reduce overfitting/selection bias and diagnose performance decay across chronological stages. It also reports that post-validation ranking of four alpha variants can reverse when the objective changes from Sharpe to maximum drawdown, illustrating that “best” depends on the chosen risk objective.

## Limitations / caveats

- The paper's numeric plateau, sample, fold and veto settings are paper-specific.
- One empirical case study does not establish universal thresholds or universal superiority.
- `Deploy / Reject / Refactor` is the paper's vocabulary, not EA_LAB governance.
- The source does not close EA_LAB `KINT-001`.
- A purge gap is not meaningful until lookback/state contamination for a concrete strategy is specified.
