# Cross-impact cost is an execution problem, not a pairs alpha

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Wang (2017) extends a market-impact execution framework from one stock to a pair of stocks. The paired execution is described by each stock's trading rate and the ratio of their trading periods.

The objective is to minimize potential cost from cross-impact: a price change in one stock caused by trading the other. The paper also applies the model to an empirical case where traded-volume impact and time-lag cross-impact are estimated for cost computation.

## Method / context

- Primary locator: arXiv `1701.03098`; SSRN `2897711`.
- Market / asset: paired equities.
- Mechanism: execution scheduling under cross-impact, not spread discovery.
- Evidence checked: primary/publication abstract and metadata.

## Result

The source supports the proposition that execution of two related legs can create interaction costs that should be modeled when they are economically material. It does not establish a new pairs-trading alpha by itself.

## Limitations / caveats

The paper concerns equity execution and cross-impact. EA_LAB has no evidence here that the owner's retail-sized MT5 orders create measurable market cross-impact. Broker spread, slippage, asynchronous leg fill and financing may be much more material in the intended environment.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

Use this source as an execution-cost decomposition prompt for a future pairs simulator: separate signal edge from two-leg implementation cost and measure leg timing explicitly. Do not invent a market-impact penalty unless it is observable or conservatively scenario-tested.

This is a downstream realism layer for `HYP-SB-003`, not a reason to add execution complexity before the deterministic pair relationship and net-cost baseline exist.
