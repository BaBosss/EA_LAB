# Rebalancing is an optimization between tracking error and trading cost
## SOURCE_CLAIM
Cesari and Marzo review portfolio monitoring and rebalancing when transaction and monitoring costs make continuous target restoration suboptimal. Their framework replaces a single exact target with a no-trade region bounded by thresholds; rebalancing is triggered when the cost of remaining away from target outweighs the cost of adjustment.
## Method / context
- Dynamic portfolio-management theory and rebalancing literature
- Distinguishes passive price drift, target changes and portfolio rebalancing
- Emphasizes transaction costs and tracking-error trade-offs
- Notes that popular rebalancing rules can incur excess costs and that rebalancing can benefit from mean-reverting regimes under some conditions
## Limitations / caveats
This is portfolio-allocation theory, not evidence for an individual EA entry signal. Exact boundaries depend on objectives, costs, risk preferences and asset dynamics.
## EA_LAB_INFERENCE
Portfolio-level strategy orchestration should consider threshold/no-trade logic rather than mechanically forcing exact allocation at every observation. This is future portfolio research only and does not change current sizing, risk or deployment policy.
