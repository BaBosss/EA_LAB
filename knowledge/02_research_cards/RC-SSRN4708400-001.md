# TSI-based short-horizon mean reversion on SPY and QQQ

## SOURCE_CLAIM

The paper studies a long-only mean-reversion system centered on True Strength Index (TSI) oversold conditions for SPY and QQQ, with RSI and other technical indicators used as supporting filters. Signals are formed at the close and positions are initiated or liquidated at the next market open.

Historical data are split into in-sample 2000-2018 and out-of-sample 1996-1999 plus 2019-2022, with paper trading beginning in 2023. The study characterizes the targeted price moves as short, averaging about five days.

Reported aggregate 1996-2023 TSI-model metrics include 17.36% annual return, -14.87% maximum drawdown and Sharpe 1.43, compared with 7.53% annual return, -56.47% maximum drawdown and Sharpe 0.55 for SPY buy-and-hold. These are source-reported backtest/paper-trading results, not independently replicated here.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: SPY and QQQ ETFs
- Timeframe / horizon: daily signals; short holding horizon, described as about five days on average
- Sample period: 1996-2022 historical evaluation; paper trading from January 2023
- Method: TSI mean-reversion trigger with RSI and additional technical filters; IS/OOS split and walk-forward tables
- Comparison / benchmark: SPY buy-and-hold and different sample windows
## Result

Within the study's specified ETF universe and test design, the TSI-centered system reports positive risk-adjusted performance across its stated in-sample, out-of-sample and 2023 paper-trading slices.

## Limitations / caveats

The authors explicitly caution that historical performance may not persist, that the method may be sensitive to TSI/RSI parameters, and that historical technical indicators do not fully represent unforeseen fundamental or market-structure changes. The study is concentrated on SPY/QQQ; adaptation to associated futures is suggested rather than established by the reported evidence. Backtest quality remains bounded by the historical data and execution assumptions.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

This source supports a future bounded hypothesis that short-horizon oversold mean reversion can be tested as a distinct mechanism family, but it does not justify importing the paper's parameters, combining indicators automatically, or accepting an EA.
