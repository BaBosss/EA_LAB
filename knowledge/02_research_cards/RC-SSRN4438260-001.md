# Trend following is a four-part system: universe, signal, portfolio construction and realistic backtest
## SOURCE_CLAIM
Broadfoot and Leveau describe a trend-following build process using a liquid multi-asset futures universe, trend detection, inverse-volatility portfolio construction and a backtest intended to mimic live trading. Their example uses weekly rebalancing, a 2% rebalance threshold and a 5 bps bid-ask spread.
## Method / context
- Example universe: 52 futures across equities, fixed income, FX and commodities
- Signal example: 60-day directional trend measure
- Sizing: inverse volatility plus portfolio volatility target
- Reported example: 4.1% average net return, 5.1% realized volatility, -10.3% max drawdown and Sharpe 0.81 over the stated long backtest
## Result

The source's illustrative diversified-futures trend strategy reports about 4.1% average net return, 5.1% realized volatility, -10.3% maximum drawdown, and Sharpe 0.81 over its stated backtest, while also identifying range-bound markets as a recurring weakness of trend following.

## Limitations / caveats
The source is a practitioner guide and its signal-quality discussion includes in-sample evidence. It also explicitly notes that trend following struggles in range-bound markets. Results from diversified futures portfolios do not directly transfer to one retail MT5 symbol/timeframe.
## EA_LAB_INFERENCE
A trend strategy should be synthesized as signal + sizing + execution + regime assumptions, not as an isolated indicator. Range/chop exposure should be an explicit adversarial test.
