# Benchmark-time liquidity can alter order flow and price impact

## SOURCE_CLAIM

Saakvitne develops a competitive-market model in which some liquidity traders prefer execution at a benchmark price, motivated by structures such as the FX 4pm London fix. The model predicts that aggregate order flow peaks when the benchmark is set while price impact reaches a minimum, equivalently market depth reaches a maximum.

The model also produces temporary price movement at benchmark time followed by a rebound. The paper emphasizes that such a pattern can arise in a rational competitive equilibrium and therefore is not, by itself, proof of manipulation.

The market structure can create incentives for manipulative frontrunning strategies, but the modeled rational market maker partly negates their effectiveness.

## Method / context

- Market / asset: theoretical competitive market motivated by FX benchmark trading
- Timeframe / horizon: before, during and after a benchmark-setting interval
- Sample period: theoretical model with empirical motivation from FX benchmark patterns
- Method: Kyle-style market model extended with benchmark-linked dealer incentives and learning market maker
- Comparison / benchmark: benchmark-time versus surrounding periods
## Result

The source supports the proposition that scheduled benchmark mechanics can change both liquidity concentration and short-horizon price-impact patterns, so execution conditions may be time-of-event dependent.

## Limitations / caveats

The paper is primarily a theoretical equilibrium model. Its FX benchmark motivation does not establish a transferable trading edge for other markets or brokers. A temporary price move and rebound around a benchmark must not be treated as sufficient evidence of manipulation or as a guaranteed mean-reversion signal.

## EA_LAB_INFERENCE

Future execution-realism work may treat known session/benchmark windows as candidate liquidity regimes and test spread, slippage and fill behavior separately. This card does not authorize frontrunning logic, runtime scheduling, or production execution changes.
