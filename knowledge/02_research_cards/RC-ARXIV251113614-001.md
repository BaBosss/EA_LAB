# Multi-agent alpha communication is market-dependent

Primary reference: Shi & Hollifield, arXiv:2511.13614 (2025).

## SOURCE_CLAIM

The paper studies five-agent LLM trading systems across 450 experiments spanning 21 months and compares five organizational structures ranging from isolated agents to collaborative and competitive communication.

The authors report that communication can improve performance, but the best communication structure depends on market characteristics. Competitive conversation performed best for volatile technology stocks in their experiments, collaborative conversation performed better for stable general stocks, and finance stocks were resistant to the tested communication interventions.

The paper also reports that conversation-quality scores had zero correlation with returns in the experiments.

## Method / context

- System: 5-agent LLM trading organizations.
- Experiment count: 450.
- Time span reported by source: 21 months.
- Comparison: isolated, collaborative and competitive communication structures.

## Result

The source supports the proposition that agent organization is a variable that can affect outcomes and that apparently sophisticated conversation is not a reliable proxy for trading performance.

## Limitations / caveats

- This is evidence about the paper's LLM trading setup and tested stock groups, not proof for EA_LAB workers or markets.
- Communication topology is confounded with agent behavior, prompts, models, market selection and experimental setup.
- A performance finding in an LLM trading experiment does not grant authority to use LLMs for live trading decisions.
